RiskMandate.ai

Which Agent Is It?

Think of an AI agent, assistant or developer tool. Answer a few questions and RiskMandate.ai will try to identify it, or tell you it hasn’t met yours yet. Before the reveal, say what you think it can do. Then see what it can reach, what it cannot, and which of it could not be undone.

Every other approach asks you to enumerate what your agent can do, which is exactly the thing you do not know. So the questions are cheap ones with obvious answers — a terminal or a browser, ever clicked always allow, can it read your mail — and the rest is inferred. The prediction you make before the reveal is the measurement.

Deterministic: arithmetic over a published tree and the public profiles, reproducible and auditable, running in this tab. No model, no server, nothing sent.

the prediction · the experiment

Before we show you: which of these can it do?

Tick every family you think its grant contains. Without this step the game is a lookup with a costume on.

the reveal, and the gap

The tuple this page would submit, and does not

Answers, the profile matched, the prediction, the gap. No address, no identifier, no free text, no timestamp finer than a day. Shown so you can see exactly what it contains; declined by default, and there is no send. To contribute it, open a pull request under probes/submissions/.


  





The belief, live


Answers update a belief rather than prune a branch, so a wrong answer is recoverable. The next question is always the one that splits the remaining belief most evenly. The population prior is a guess and biases the first questions toward the common case; it says so in the tree file.




The path — a tree nobody can inspect is a fortune teller

A belief, a measurement, and the distance between them as the product. Two words, kept apart on purpose: a surprise is an action outside the grant — a measurement failure, and the assessment's validity test; a prediction gap is what a person did not expect their grant to contain. The game measures the second and never reports the first.



What this does not prove


    

  • Anything about your environment. It matched a profile from your answers — the weakest tier available — and produced a hypothesis about your grant. The probes produce the measurement.
    • 

  • That the tree is right. Seven profiles and fifteen questions, with probabilities estimated by the author on one day and answered by nobody yet. A wrong branch is a contribution, and “it hasn’t met yours yet” is the honest answer when no profile dominates.
    • 

  • That a small gap is safety. Predicting the grant correctly does not narrow it; the reduction on the reveal screen does.
    • 

  • Anything at scale. No tuple has been submitted; the aggregate is empty, and will be published either way.
    • 



Working title guess the agent; named Which Agent Is It? by the project lead on 5 September, for the question a person has rather than the mechanic. Specified by brief v0.33.64 (guess the agent); steps 1 to 5 of its build order are what is here, the model-assisted fallback (step 7) is deliberately not, because a tree's failure is a recorded gap and a model's is a plausible guess.
