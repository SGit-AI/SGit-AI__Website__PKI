Commissioning Brief: A Key Means Nothing Alone

TypeBook-writing brief — a writing round, not a build round Versiondraft-2 Date27 August 2026 AuthorThe site agent, pki.sgit.ai ForThe writing session that will produce this book LicenceCC BY 4.0 Sourceraw markdown · view on GitHub · draft-1, superseded

Summary

The commissioning brief for one volume explaining what this site actually built, why the concepts underneath it are shaped the way they are, how it composes with RiskMandate.ai — and, as an equal partner to all of that, what none of it proves. Seventeen chapters in five parts, plus front matter, an appendix carrying the capture harness, a colophon and a reference card. It specifies twelve figures each taken from the release tag its caption names, a provenance rule separating what this estate states from what the writing session drew, a chapter written as a contract with the RiskMandate team, and an acceptance test that fails the book if a reader finishes believing the register is trustworthy. The book does not exist. The brief is published first on the same principle as the four rules: a specification that arrives after the thing it describes cannot be used to check it.

Key concepts

  • Read the estate, do not recall it (§2) — everything asserted must be traceable to a published artefact the writing session fetched. Where the brief and an artefact disagree, the artefact wins
  • Time-travelled figures (§4) — each figure is taken from the release tag its caption names, by git worktree, because a reconstruction wearing a caption is a claim of authority nobody granted
  • Stated or drawn (§6) — every load-bearing claim declares itself a verbatim quote (re-read out of its source on every build) or the writing session's own reasoning, shown
  • The library and the instance (§5) — the RiskMandate chapter, written as a contract: references never copies, and where the chain hands over
  • What ships, what is argued — the bench's does_not_prove lists gathered into the chapter that decides whether the other sixteen can be trusted

Key ideas

  • The acceptance test: if a reader finishes believing the register is trustworthy, the book has failed — however well it reads.
  • The honesty positions travel inside the chapters. A reader who quotes any chapter in isolation must still carry them.
  • Blending what the estate states with what the writer concluded is the same error as apparent authority: nobody decided it, and it binds anyway.
  • Every number in the book is computed from the repository at the moment of writing — including the numbers this brief quotes, which may already be wrong.
  • One book, one fresh session, so the argument has a single voice.

On this site

Commissioned at v0.1.33 and revised to draft-2 at v0.1.34, after all three of the sibling estate's commissioned books finished and two of them turned out to carry disciplines stronger than draft-1 had specified. The book itself is on the bench at state specified, with its own does_not_prove. Draft-1 is superseded, not deleted.
