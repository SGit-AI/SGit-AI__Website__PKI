## What This Is

**Every one of the eleven insurance readings was re-read against the transcript it came from**, and the four load-bearing corpus quotations were re-verified out of the pre-pivot files they cite. **The method held. The arithmetic and the housekeeping did not.** Six defects were found: three stale counts, one claim identified with the wrong quantity, one over-claim, and one dropped question. All six are fixed, and a build gate now refuses the class of defect that produced half of them.

## The Verdict First

**The interpretation is sound and I agree with it.** The things that would have been most damaging to get wrong were got right:

- **The two-stage split** — memo 1 does decouple the rating from the money, in those words, and everything downstream depends on that being a real separation rather than a convenient one.
- **The three brokers** ([doctrine 05](../insurance/not-in-line.html) §3). Memo 5's *"our job is to be the broker, right? Not the broker"* is the **insurance** broker. Memo 6's brokers are **access and execution** brokers — technology products, a different referent for the same word one memo later. Conflating them would have produced a doctrine that contradicted itself and looked consistent. It was caught.
- **The Fibonacci repair** ([doctrine 08](../insurance/the-world-model.html) §3). *"a finibash in a finibashy sequence"* is **Fibonacci**, and the reading's split — *what the bands are called* is settled at 1–5, *what the bands represent* is open — saves a settled decision from a good instinct that appeared to contradict it.
- **The 2D-first sequencing** disagrees with the memo, which explicitly asks for 3D. It is labelled as the site agent's engineering advice and carried into `does_not_prove`. That is the disagreement handled correctly.
- **All four corpus quotations verify verbatim**, at the dates claimed: *a signature proves possession* (19 Aug), *a verification is not a use* (20 Aug), the revocation-latency line (20 Aug), *trust is a path and revocation is that path no longer existing* (27 Aug).

## The Six Defects

### 1 · Three stale counts, and the pattern behind them

| Where | Said | True |
|---|---|---|
| [Doctrine 00](../insurance/what-this-is.html) | *"Eight memos are being recorded"* | Ten, and complete |
| Doctrine 01–08 footers | *"memo N of 8"* | of 10 |
| [Doctrine 08](../insurance/the-world-model.html), twice | memo 8 *"the last of the series"* | Memos 9 and 10 followed |

**This is the third time this class of defect has appeared in this folder**, after the hub counter reading *8 of 8* and `llms.txt` printing *seventy-one decisions* against a log of eighty. The common cause is the same each time: **a number typed into prose by a writer who knew it, in a folder whose own rule is that a claim must be checkable.**

So the fix is not the three edits. **`gen_insurance.py` now gates it** (GM-D81): a doctrine document's *memo N of M* is checked against the manifest, and only the highest-numbered memo may be called the last one.

> **The gate caught real text on its first run** — the correction note in doctrine 08, which recorded the false claim by restating it. The note was reworded rather than the gate loosened.

### 2 · The tier was identified with the wrong quantity

**The most consequential finding.** [Doctrine 04](../insurance/why-insurance-and-what-broke-it.html) §3 took memo 4's observation — an incident response team reduces the *impact* of incidents, not their number — and answered it with the enforcement tier, claiming the estate *"has been computing that quantity since v0.1.28 without naming it."*

**It has not.** Insurance splits a loss into **how often** and **how bad**. The incident-response team is squarely the second: the incident happened, and the team makes it smaller. The tier is **neither** — attempts are unchanged, but fewer of them succeed. That is closer to frequency than to severity.

> **The tier ranks reachability. That is blast-radius reduction, not severity reduction** (GM-D82, narrowing GM-D52).

Still the useful half, because for an agent placement the blast radius *is* most of the severity. **But it is a proxy, and the document now says so** rather than claiming the memo's quantity under a different name. A rating built on the original wording would have credited a boundary control for post-incident recovery capability it has nothing to do with.

### 3 · An over-claim of immunity, contradicted three documents later

[Doctrine 02](../insurance/the-ecosystem-and-the-gate.html) §5 said stage 1 is **structurally immune** to moral hazard, because there is no payout to offload into.

**Doctrine 09 contradicts it without noticing.** Its guard-rail — *make your agents insurable must not become make your agents look insurable* — is moral hazard with no carrier anywhere near it. A level is a badge, and *we are a level 2* is available as a reason to stop looking.

> **Removing the payout removes one channel and opens another** (GM-D84). The rating's channel is cheaper to police, because a derivation anybody can recompute is a badge anybody can dispute — which is why the rule in doctrine 00 is not optional.

### 4 · A question dropped between brief and doctrine

Memo 3 asks: *"is Claude that actually owns that risk — is Claude going to actually have a policy on its own to cover any mistakes?"* [Brief v0.33.74](v0.33.74__strategy-brief__who-pays-for-the-delta-nobody-chose-and-the-rating-that-moves-overnight.md) §9 answered it. **Doctrine 03 dropped it entirely.**

That is a real loss, because [doctrine 06's](../insurance/the-broker-market.html) central claim — a broker asserting *we reduce your level* must carry its own policy or the claim is unbacked marketing — **is the same argument, and memo 3 made it three memos earlier about a different party.** The doctrine read as though memo 6 invented it.

Restored as [doctrine 03](../insurance/who-pays-and-the-moving-rating.html) §8, with the generalisation the two memos jointly support:

> **Any party claiming to reduce somebody else's exposure should carry cover against being wrong about it** (GM-D83). Broker, platform, or model vendor — the argument does not care which.

Flagged as speculation about the vendors, who disclaim liability today. **The structural point is not speculation**: a vendor offering such cover would be making the first falsifiable quality claim in the category, because a benchmark is not a number anybody pays when they are wrong.

### 5 · A forward reference that never landed

[Brief v0.33.73](v0.33.73__strategy-brief__the-ecosystem-without-the-money-insurance-as-a-go-live-gate.md) flagged memo 2's ambiguous *"rider"* and promised: *"the endorsement reading turns out to describe something this estate has already done, in §6."* **Section 6 is about the control-to-premium loop and never mentions endorsements.**

The promise was worth keeping, because the reading is right. An endorsement amends cover after issue without rewriting the policy — which is the register's rule since v0.1.26:

> **An endorsement is an append** (GM-D85). The estate held the endorsement mechanism before it had a policy to attach one to.

[Doctrine 10](../insurance/the-schemas-and-the-clocks.html) reached the same place from memo 10 — *amending, activating, ending: appends, never edits* — three memos later and independently. **Now delivered in [doctrine 02](../insurance/the-ecosystem-and-the-gate.html) §7**, where it was promised.

### 6 · One transcription artefact never flagged

Every memo in the series had its artefacts flagged when it was read — *redempting* → indemnity, *instance response* → incident response, *finibash* → Fibonacci, *rider* discussed at length. **Memo 3's was missed.** It reads *"the risks that exists outside the main"* in a passage whose subject is what happens outside the **mandate**; the sentence before it names *"the authorised one, i.e. the mandates."*

Flagged in doctrine 03 §1, **not repaired in the transcript**, per the rule that the memo is the source.

## One Correction That Was Only Half Made

[Doctrine 06](../insurance/the-broker-market.html) opens by announcing it corrects doctrine 03 §6. **Doctrine 03 §6 carried no notice that it had been corrected**, so a reader arriving there got the superseded claim with nothing to warn them. A superseded notice now stands in both places.

> **A correction only the correcting document knows about is not a correction.**

## What This Audit Did Not Do

- **It did not touch a transcript.** Not one word of any memo was changed. The artefact in §6 is flagged in the doctrine, not repaired at source.
- **It did not re-open settled decisions.** GM-D54 is the project lead's and stands untouched.
- **It did not check the readings against the world.** Every defect found here is internal — an arithmetic error, a category error, a dropped thread. **The one thing that could establish whether any of this is right about insurance is still the survey, and it still has not been run.**
- **It did not audit the pre-pivot corpus.** Only the four quotations the insurance documents rely on were verified, not the documents they came from.
