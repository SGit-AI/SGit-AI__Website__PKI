The Eleven Readings, Audited Against Their Transcripts

TypeAudit brief — no memo behind it Versionv0.33.82 Date31 August 2026 AuthorDinis Cruz (project lead) and collaborators LicenceCC BY 4.0 Sourceraw markdown · view on GitHub

Summary

The site agent auditing the site agent, at the project lead's request: every one of the eleven insurance readings re-read against the transcript it came from, and the four load-bearing corpus quotations re-verified out of the pre-pivot files they cite. The verdict is that the method held and the arithmetic did not. What was got right was the expensive stuff — the two-stage split, the three different things one word was covering when memo 5 said 'not the broker' about an insurance broker and memo 6 meant execution brokers, the Fibonacci repair that saves a settled decision from an instinct that appeared to contradict it, and the 2D-first sequencing labelled honestly as a disagreement with the memo. All four quotations verify verbatim at the dates claimed. Six defects were found and fixed. Three were stale counts, which is the third appearance of that class in this folder, so the fix is a build gate rather than three edits — and the gate caught real text on its first run. One was a category error: the enforcement tier was identified with the incident-response team's quantity, and insurance splits a loss into how often and how bad, of which the tier is neither. One was an over-claim of structural immunity to moral hazard that doctrine 09 contradicts without noticing, because a level is a badge and a badge substitutes for control with no carrier in sight. One was a question dropped between brief and doctrine — memo 3 asked whether a model vendor would carry a policy against its own mistakes, three memos before the same argument was made about brokers and presented as new.

Key concepts

  • The insurance folder — the eleven documents audited
  • Why insurance, and what broke it — where the tier was identified with the wrong quantity
  • The ecosystem and the gate — the immunity over-claim, and the rider read at last
  • Who pays, and the rating that moves — the dropped question, restored
  • Change control — GM-D81 to GM-D85

Key ideas

  • The method held. The arithmetic and the housekeeping did not.
  • A number typed into prose, in a folder whose own rule is that a claim must be checkable — the third time this class of defect has appeared here, so the fix is a gate, not three edits.
  • The gate caught real text on its first run: the correction note that recorded a false claim by restating it. The note was reworded rather than the gate loosened.
  • Insurance splits a loss into how often and how bad. An incident response team is the second. The enforcement tier is neither — it changes how many attempts become losses at all.
  • The tier ranks reachability. That is blast-radius reduction, not severity reduction, and blast radius is a proxy that is weakest exactly where a small reach touches something critical.
  • Removing the payout removes one channel of moral hazard and opens another: a level is a badge, and a badge substitutes for control without a carrier anywhere near it.
  • Any party claiming to reduce somebody else's exposure should carry cover against being wrong about it — broker, platform, or model vendor.
  • A correction only the correcting document knows about is not a correction.
  • An endorsement is an append. The estate held the endorsement mechanism before it had a policy to attach one to.
  • Every defect found here is internal. The one thing that could establish whether any of this is right about insurance is still the survey, and it still has not been run.

On this site

Audits all eleven insurance readings against their transcripts and fixes six defects in place. Adds a build gate in gen_insurance.py against stale series arithmetic. Proposes GM-D81 (a printed count is derived, never typed), GM-D82 (the tier ranks reachability, narrowing GM-D52), GM-D83 (any party claiming to reduce exposure carries cover against being wrong), GM-D84 (removing the payout opens a second moral-hazard channel, correcting doctrine 02) and GM-D85 (an endorsement is an append).
